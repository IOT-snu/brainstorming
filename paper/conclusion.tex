\section{Conclusion}
A detector that learns on the device needs a second witness, and an IoT fleet provides one. FOA lets
each device take freely whatever drift its vulnerability-diverse peers also see, and rations only what
is specific to it. It has no gate that stops learning, so a freeze attack has nothing to trip; it bounds what an attacker on one
device can do and how fast; and it costs one 92-byte frame per cycle and about 22\,KB of flash on an
ESP32. Three real boards then showed what simulation had missed: identical, repetitive devices can shrink
their detectors together until almost everything alarms. A coverage floor removes that failure in
simulation and on the boards, and in doing so shows that FOA's real advantage is not fewer false alarms than a single
device but resistance to poisoning hidden inside drift. The boards also showed that a change larger than the alarm radius
leaves nothing to learn from. The next steps are attacks on the boards and a safe way to recover from
such a step. What FOA cannot do, and what no rule can, is tell a device's own honest change from an attack
on that device.
