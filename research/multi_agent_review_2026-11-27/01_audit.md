1. COMPONENTS IMPLEMENTED (with paths)
======================================================================
Detector
- experiments/foa/detector.py: centroid detector, score = ||x-mu||/s. tau is the 99th percentile of calibration scores. Two local proposal rules: "naive", and "auto" (signature-free: learns from the 80th-percentile core only, scales the step by outer-ring support, and gates spread growth). It returns a zero proposal when fewer than 5 core points exist.

FOA rule
- experiments/foa/foa_rule.py `foa_step`: geometric-median consensus, EMA (alpha 0.2), significance ramp z in [2,4], robust lambda (median projection of own core points), common budget (B0c 0.75, Bc 0.06 rho/cycle), residual budget (B0 0.1, B 0.005 rho/cycle; B0s 0.1, Bs 0.001 log-spread), and an optional coverage floor `s_floor`.
- Variants kept in the code: joint budget, mean-lambda, own-direction common channel ("foa_own", "foa_tcs").
- Docstring inconsistency: the module docstring describes ONE JOINT budget, ||R||/rho + |Rs| <= B0 + Bt. Both the paper and the default Policy (`joint=False` in fleet.py) use separate budgets. The joint budget is only an ablation.
- experiments/foa/robust.py: Weiszfeld geometric median (10 iterations from the coordinate-wise median), ball projection, and the Minsker constant.

Simulator, baselines and attackers

- experiments/foa/attacks.py: boundary, core, mimicry, multidir, aligned, decoy and pulsing poisoners; Byzantine peers; Sybil (forge/replay).

The varied-periods result is a natural experiment that supports the aliasing explanation:
- Samples arrive every 50 ms, and calibration keeps every 10th sample, i.e. one every 500 ms.
- With a 200 ms telemetry period, 500 mod 200 = 100 ms, so calibration only sees 2 of the 4 phases. With 160 or 240 ms the remainder is 20 ms, so it cycles through all phases.
- The host fixture agrees: fixed stride flagged 101 of 400 samples, rotated flagged 11 of 400.

Measurements
- Power: 381.7 to 391.2 mW mean in every phase. There was no detectable difference between warm-up, calibration and operation. INA226 read every 40 ms with about 35 ms averaging.
- Timing on the new runs: proposal 21.4 to 21.6 ms (including batch classification), step 1.6 to 1.9 ms, sign about 0.6 ms, verify about 1.0 ms. The total is about 25 ms. The paper says 15 ms in the abstract and 19 ms in section 6.

What failed and why
(a) Calibration aliasing. The fixed stride CAL_EVERY=10 (firmware/esp8266_foa/foa_config.h) is phase-locked to the 200 ms telemetry. This means every earlier hardware number, including the "90% collapse" runs and the static control, was confounded by a bad calibration. The collapse itself was real (spread 0.035 in archive floor4), but its false-alarm figure is not attributable to the ratchet alone.

(b) Abrupt drift causes a de facto freeze.
- FOA learns only from accepted core points. When a step change pushes all traffic outside the alarm radius, own proposals become zero. lambda decays because own_along = 0, and peers broadcast zero proposals too (n_core < 5 gives a zero proposal).
- Nothing moves, and the boards stayed at 100% false alarms. Peers do not help because every board drifted at once and every board's core was empty.
- This contradicts the spirit of Prop 4(ii), "no input stops FOA from learning". The residual budget admits B*rho per cycle, but only of proposals that are zero.

(c) Clean centre walk.
- In clean runs the centre moved 3.5 to 5.5 calibrated spreads. With tau about 5.4 to 5.6 (CAL lines in experiments/hardware/*/COM*.log), that is about 0.65 to 1.0 alarm radii in under 40 minutes with no attacker.

(d) Not done yet
- No attack, decoy, forgery or replay trials on the boards.
- The basis is still the PLACEHOLDER (FEAT_MEAN = 0, FEAT_STD = 1, random W in foa_config.h) with development keys.
- No repeated runs, so no confidence intervals.
- The traffic is the boards' own telemetry and UDP, which is not realistic IoT traffic.

Reproducibility gaps on the branch
- The rotated-stride, drift and no-peer firmware builds are NOT committed. The firmware on the branch is byte-identical to main.
- The raw serial logs of the 27 Nov runs and followup_20261127_142002.txt are not committed. Only RESULTS.md, the CSV, the PNG and the firmware check are.

4. GAPS, WEAK POINTS, INCONSISTENCIES, EXAMINER ATTACK SURFACE
======================================================================
4.1 Byzantine slip (paper/theory.tex)
- The Lemma as written says "if MORE than (1-alpha)K of K points lie within r of z". The proof of Thm 2 sets alpha = f/K and asserts "K-f > (1-f/K)K points lie within r_t". That is false: K-f equals (1-f/K)K exactly.
- Minsker's Lemma 2.1(a), which I read from the arXiv PDF, is stated in a Hilbert space in contrapositive form: if ||x*-z|| > C_alpha r, then there is a set J with |J| > alpha k and ||x_j - z|| > r for all j in J. Its contrapositive needs only "AT LEAST (1-alpha)K points within r". So the fix is to restate the Lemma with "at least" (or "at most alpha K points farther than r"). Then K-f = (1-f/K)K satisfies it for f < K/2.
- The same wording is in robust.py `minsker_constant`.
- Further gaps in Thm 2:
  (i) The lemma covers the exact geometric median, but FOA uses 10 Weiszfeld iterations, which give an approximation. Say so, or bound the error.
  (ii) It bounds ||m - m^H|| but not the increment. Byzantine peers also move w (through the dispersion estimate) and the direction used for lambda.
  (iii) It does not cover the common log-spread channel at all. That channel is a 1-D median with a generous budget (B0cs 0.7, Bcs 0.02/cycle), and E6/E6b traced the Byzantine-assisted compromises to exactly this channel.
  (iv) The testbed has K = 2 peers. The geometric median of two points is their midpoint, so it tolerates f = 0.
  (v) E6b uses f = 4 of 7 or 8 peers, which is outside f < K/2.

4.2 RTTAD wording (paper/related.tex)
- The paper says "RTTAD updates only from confidently normal points and stops otherwise".
- The full text (arXiv 2605.10242, Huang, Qiao, Zhang, Ye, Shang, Fu, 2026) says something different:
  - TTCL selects BOTH high-confidence pseudo-normal samples (pulled toward normal) AND high-confidence pseudo-anomalous samples (pushed away, with a negative sign in Eqs. 11 and 12).
  - Ambiguous samples are excluded.
  - Rounds end "until no sufficient samples remain". It is batch, tabular and has no attacker. There is no confidence rule that halts adaptation.
- Suggested text: "RTTAD adapts at test time on high-confidence pseudo-normal and pseudo-anomalous samples, ignores ambiguous ones, and iterates until too few confident samples remain."
- Also soften "Each has a condition under which it stops learning" for RTTAD.
- The viva prep answer (demo/build_viva_prep.py:136) is already accurate. Slide 14 in demo/build_final_deck.py is not: it omits the pseudo-anomalous half.

4.3 DriftXpert baseline is inverted
- DriftXpert (arXiv 2609.08623, Zha, Kang, Liu, Shen, Zhang, 2026) compares an outlier ratio with a threshold T to DETECT drift, and then TRIGGERS a model update. I found no pause rule in the text I read.
- Our "or" baseline (fleet.py) SKIPS updates while the alarm rate exceeds 3x its moving average, and the paper and slides call it "DriftXpert style".
- An examiner can say the freeze comparison misrepresents DriftXpert. Rename it "outlier-ratio pause gate (our construction)", or implement DriftXpert's trigger logic.

4.4 TinyML survey claim
- paper/related.tex says the survey "found no security mechanism among them". The slides say the same.
- I read the abstract and the HTML text of arXiv 2605.31226 (Pavan, Pezzarossa, Pittorino, Roveri, Fafoutis, 2026). It does not discuss security, and I found no sentence saying the works lack it.
- Reword to "the survey does not cover security or poisoning", or drop the claim. research/references.json marks it as "full text" read, which overstates this.

4.5 Paper and slides are stale against the hardware branch
- hardware.tex and the slides say "the floored rule has not yet been run on the boards". It now has: spread held at 1.0.
- The new findings (calibration aliasing, abrupt-drift failure, 21 to 25 ms timing, 381 to 391 mW, ESP8266 footprint with 93% IRAM) are not in the paper. The branch did not touch paper/.
- The paper attributes the 90% FPR to the spread ratchet. The static control shows that about 46% came from calibration alone.

4.6 Internal numeric inconsistencies
- Compute per cycle: abstract "about 15 ms", hardware.tex "about 19 ms", table 11.2 + 1.6 + 0.6 + 0.95 = 14.4 ms, branch about 25 ms.
- Board power: 365 mW in the paper vs 381 to 391 mW now.
- Tamper test: the cost table says "0 of 23,104" and the text says "23,040 single-bit flips".
- Keywords and contributions say ESP32, but the hardware is ESP8266. The footprint (cost.tex) is ESP32-only.
- Intro and abstract still lead with the FPR advantage (9.0% vs 24.6%), which E12 shows is an artefact. Only section 6.3 and the end of the abstract correct it.
- bib.tex: Korycki and Krawczyk is Machine Learning 112:4013 to 4048, 2023 (the bib says 2022). The authors of the 2026 preprints are now known and can be added.

4.7 Security design weaknesses in the firmware
- Every node stores every node's symmetric key (FOA_KEYS[N_NODES][32] in foa_config.h, used as FOA_KEYS[snd]). Capturing one board therefore yields all keys. The attacker can impersonate all peers, so f is no longer below K/2 and Thm 2 no longer applies.
- The threat model assumes per-node keys that are not shared. This is the classic broadcast-MAC problem that TESLA addresses. Development keys are also committed in the public source.
- Replay window: freshness resets when a peer has been silent for PEER_RESET_MS = 60 s (esp8266_foa.ino:51, 228). An attacker who jams a peer for 60 s can replay its old frames. E6 only modelled previous-cycle replays.
- Peers' n_core is sent in every frame but never used. Peers with no learning data still vote with a zero vector.

4.8 What the guarantees mean in practice
- Cor 1 in wall-clock time: t* is about (M - 1 - B0 - B0s)/(B + Bs) = (M - 1.2)/0.006 cycles.
  - For victims at M = 1.3 to 1.9 alarm radii, that is 17 to 117 cycles: about 2 to 20 minutes at 7.8 to 10 s per cycle.
  - It also excludes the common channel, whose budget is (0.75 + 0.06t) rho, i.e. 6.75 rho after 100 cycles.
  - "Bounded" is true. "Safe for long" rests on the empirical results, not the bound.
- Monoculture result is by construction: with "diverse" peer selection no infected same-vendor camera is ever a peer, so 0% is guaranteed. The paper admits that BASHLITE infected all nine N-BaIoT devices.
- Victim rule: victim margins are measured on the same held-out attack traffic that later scores compromise. It is defensible (it selects victims, not tuning), but expect the question.
- testbed_sim.txt shows 0/20 compromised in every condition, including the old firmware. That simulation cannot tell the methods apart and should not be used as evidence.
- Synthetic elements: fleet concurrency on N-BaIoT, the drift (feature transforms), the poisoner (feature vectors, not packets), the CESNET attack (modelled scanner; aggregated research-network IPs, not IoT devices), and the hardware traffic (the boards' own telemetry).
- E6b's Byzantine-plus-poisoner result (56% at f=3) is a real limit. The paper reports it honestly.
- Process: the independent code review planned for 24 Oct never ran (RESEARCH_LOG.md).
- Privacy: an uncommitted change in demo/build_final_deck.py now uses research.jpeg (unblurred) by default instead of the blurred demo/testbed_photo.jpg.

5. DOES SOMETHING LIKE THIS EXIST? (all found in this run)
======================================================================
We found no prior work that combines all of the following:
- per-device on-device adaptation,
- a consensus of vulnerability-diverse peers used only for the common component,
- a never-closing budget on the device-only residual,
- a freeze analysis of stop-learning gates.

My search was a dozen queries, not a systematic review. The closest work:
- CIoTA: T. Golomb, Y. Mirsky, Y. Elovici, NDSS DISS workshop 2018. https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03807. Journal version: Y. Mirsky, T. Golomb, Y. Elovici, "Lightweight Collaborative Anomaly Detection for the IoT using Blockchain", J. Parallel and Distributed Computing 145, 2020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10587. Same-model consensus via self-attestation. Already cited (2018 only); cite the journal version too.
- DIoT: T. D. Nguyen, S. Marchal, M. Miettinen, H. Fereidooni, N. Asokan, A.-R. Sadeghi, IEEE ICDCS 2019. https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.07474. Federated, per-device-type anomaly detection for IoT. Not cited.
- T. D. Nguyen, P. Rieger, M. Miettinen, A.-R. Sadeghi, "Poisoning Attacks on Federated Learning-based IoT Intrusion Detection System", NDSS DISS workshop 2020. https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/diss2020-23003-paper.pdf. Compromised IoT devices gradually inject malicious traffic, which is essentially our threat model in a federated setting. MISSING; must cite.
- CADeSH: Y. Meidan, D. Avraham, H. Libhaber, A. Shabtai, IEEE Internet of Things Journal 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.01021. Collaborative anomaly detection across homes and identical devices. Not cited.
- RFA: K. Pillutla, S. M. Kakade, Z. Harchaoui, "Robust Aggregation for Federated Learning", IEEE Trans. Signal Processing 70, 2022. https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.13445. Geometric-median aggregation, the same primitive FOA uses.
- ClippedGossip: L. He, S. P. Karimireddy, M. Jaggi, "Byzantine-Robust Decentralized Learning via ClippedGossip", arXiv 2202.01545 [preprint; venue not verified]. Projects received parameters onto a ball around the node's own parameters, close to FOA's budget idea in a gossip setting.
- FLTrust: X. Cao, M. Fang, J. Liu, N. Z. Gong, NDSS 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13995. Direction-based trust plus magnitude rescaling, analogous to lambda.
- Kloft and Laskov, "Security Analysis of Online Centroid Anomaly Detection", JMLR 13, 2012. https://jmlr.org/papers/v13/kloft12b.html. Already cited. The same detector, with bounds on attacker gain.
- Korycki and Krawczyk, Machine Learning 112, 2023. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-022-06177-w. Poisoning drift detectors.
- F. Hinder, V. Vaquet, B. Hammer, "Adversarial Attacks for Drift Detection", ESANN 2025 (accepted per the arXiv comments). https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.16591. Drift that standard detectors miss. Not cited.
- WARDEN: V. B. Balachandran, arXiv 2607.21673, 2026 [preprint]. Self-poisoning threshold and a frozen-reserve admission gate.
- Hu and Duan, "Theory of Continual Learning Against Data Poisoning Attacks", arXiv 2606.29841, 2026 [preprint]. Its rollback is triggered by a parameter-update deviation score against a rolling mean. Our wording is acceptable.
- Rapid Poison: D. Huang, J. Chang, A. Shah, P. Mittal, C. Sitawarin, arXiv 2606.16242; ICML 2026 spotlight per the arXiv comments.
- Broadcast authentication: TESLA, A. Perrig, R. Canetti, J. D. Tygar, D. Song; RFC 4082 (2005). https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4082. Symmetric MACs cannot authenticate broadcast to multiple receivers, which is the fix for 4.7.
- Espressif ESP-NOW docs: encryption of broadcast frames is not supported. https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-idf/en/latest/api-reference/network/esp_now.html. This confirms the paper's claim.
